# RoPE position scaling: what each method does to the spectrum

Generated by `python scripts/generate_results.py`. Do not edit by hand.

Every number on this page is closed-form algebra over the RoPE frequency
ladder -- no model, no sampling, no timing. It is identical on any machine
to the last digit, which is why absolute values are committed here and
only orderings are committed for the evaluation.

- Geometry: head_dim 64, base 1000000, assumed training context 4096
  (Qwen2.5-0.5B-Instruct, so the analysis describes the model evaluated)
- YaRN ramp bounds: alpha 1.0 turn, beta 32.0 turns
- Reproduce: `python scripts/generate_results.py`

## 1. Why extrapolation fails and interpolation is even possible

`theta_i = base ** (-2i/D)`, so pair `i` completes a full turn every
`2*pi/theta_i` tokens. Two facts about the ends of that ladder set up
everything the scaling methods do.

- Fastest pair: wavelength 6.28 tokens. Inside a 4096-token
  training window it completes **652 full turns**. Only its
  phase can carry information; its turn count cannot.
- Slowest pair: wavelength 4,080,185 tokens -- longer than the training
  context, so it completes **0.001 of one turn**. It has never
  been observed past that fraction of its period.

Those two lines are the whole design space. The slow pairs are the ones
that genuinely run out of range, and the fast pairs are the ones that
carry local resolution. A method is good exactly insofar as it spends its
distortion on the former and spares the latter.

The orderings this script asserts, at every scale in the sweep:

1. `pi` retains exactly `1/s` of the fastest frequency -- the most
   local-resolution damage of any method, by construction.
2. `ntk` and `yarn` retain exactly `1.0000` of it. For NTK this is not
   tuning: `theta_0 = base**0 = 1` for *any* base, so changing the base
   provably cannot move the fastest pair.
3. All three repairs converge on `1/s` at the slowest pair. There is no
   disagreement about the low frequencies -- that is where the range has
   to come from. The methods differ only in what they charge for it.

So NTK-aware scaling is not a better guess than position interpolation.
It is the same total interpolation, redistributed onto the pairs that
were not carrying local information anyway. That redistribution is why
it works without fine-tuning while PI needs it.

## 3. YaRN's ramp: the split made explicit

NTK redistributes smoothly because a power law happens to be smooth.
YaRN makes the decision a rule: classify pair `i` by `r_i =
train_ctx / wavelength_i`, the turns it completes in training.

- **7** of 32 pairs complete >= 32 turns: not
  interpolated at all.
- **16** pairs complete <= 1 turn: interpolated fully, as PI would.
- **9** pairs sit in the linear ramp between the two.

The partition is the contribution. NTK's curve never fully stops
interpolating the fast pairs and never fully commits on the slow ones;
YaRN's does both, and only ramps across the genuinely ambiguous middle.

### The temperature term

| scale | t = 0.1*ln(s)+1 | logits multiplied by |
|---|---|---|
| 2x | 1.0693 | 0.9352 |
| 4x | 1.1386 | 0.8782 |
| 8x | 1.2079 | 0.8279 |
| 16x | 1.2773 | 0.7829 |
| 32x | 1.3466 | 0.7426 |

Spreading positions over a wider range lowers the expected magnitude of
the attention logits, so softmax flattens and the model attends less
sharply. YaRN corrects it with a scalar. This is the term most
reimplementations drop, because it is the one part of YaRN that is not
a frequency edit -- and dropping it silently removes a real fraction of
the method. A test in `tests/test_scaling.py` asserts it is present and
that `none`, `pi` and `ntk` leave it at exactly 1.0.

## 4. What this section does not establish

These are properties of the *encoding*, not of any trained model. A
method that preserves high-frequency structure perfectly on paper can
still lose to another after fine-tuning, and nothing here measures
perplexity or downstream accuracy under scaling. Establishing that needs
a fine-tuning budget this repo does not have -- see `Limitations` in the
README. LongRoPE is omitted for the same reason: its per-dimension
rescale is found by evolutionary search against a validation corpus, not
by a closed form, so it cannot join a table of exact algebra.
