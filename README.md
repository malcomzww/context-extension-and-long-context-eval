# Context extension and long-context evaluation

**Needle-in-a-haystack scores 100% at every length this repo measured. A
two-hop retrieval task on the *same haystack* — same filler, same lengths,
same answer format, same scorer, one extra hop — scores 17% and 8%.**

That gap is the deliverable. It is the reason "effective context" cannot be
reported as one number per model: NIAH answers "does the positional encoding
still resolve at this length", and it gets read as "can the model use this
much context". Those are different questions with different answers.

**This repo answers one question:** how far short of advertised context does
effective context actually fall? Scope is deliberately narrow — RoPE and the
three standard position-scaling methods implemented from the rotation
formulation, plus two retrieval tasks that differ by exactly one hop. No
training, no fine-tuning, no serving. Anything that does not help answer that
question is out of scope.

> **Distinct from the attention sibling.**
> [`attention-kv-cache-from-scratch`](../attention-kv-cache-from-scratch)
> implements `softmax(QK^T/√D)V`, the MHA/MQA/GQA head layouts and the KV
> cache. This repo implements what happens to `q` and `k` *before* that dot
> product — position encoding, and what breaks when you ask for positions the
> model never trained on. No attention machinery is reimplemented here.

## Why this exists

RoPE scaling is usually explained as three named recipes to pick from. That
framing hides the thing worth knowing: all three are the *same* intervention —
an edit to the frequency vector — and they differ only in which RoPE
dimensions they are willing to damage. Once written that way the comparison
becomes exact algebra rather than a matter of preference, and one result falls
out immediately: **NTK-aware scaling is not a better guess than position
interpolation. It is the same total interpolation, redistributed onto the
dimensions that were not carrying local information anyway.**

The evaluation half exists because the standard instrument is too easy. NIAH
is single-hop lexical retrieval — the question names a city, exactly one line
in the document names that city, and induction heads solve it. Measuring
long-context capability with it and reporting the result as "effective
context" overstates what the model can do, and this repo measures by how much.

### Effective versus advertised context

`Qwen/Qwen2.5-0.5B-Instruct`, float32, greedy decoding, seed 0, CPU.
Advertised context 32,768 tokens. Pass bar 50%, fixed before the run.
12 samples per cell; intervals are Wilson 95%.

| context | NIAH | 95% CI | multi-hop | 95% CI | gap |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1,024 | 100% | [76%, 100%] | 17% | [5%, 45%] | +83 pp |
| 2,048 | 100% | [76%, 100%] | 8% | [1%, 35%] | +92 pp |

| definition | length | fraction of advertised |
|---|---|---|
| advertised (`max_position_embeddings`) | 32,768 | 100% |
| longest length where NIAH passes | 2,048 | 6.2% |
| longest length where multi-hop passes | never passes | n/a |

The confidence intervals are disjoint at both lengths, so the gap is not a
sampling artefact. Because one hop is the only difference between the two
prompts, it is also not an artefact of prompt style or grader leniency.

### The honest reading of that gap

**The separation is already present at the shortest length measured, so it is
mostly a task-difficulty floor rather than a context effect.** At 1,024
tokens context length is not plausibly the binding constraint, and multi-hop
is already at 17%. A 0.5B model largely cannot compose two facts *at any
length*.

So what this sweep establishes is the narrower claim: **NIAH and two-hop
retrieval measure different things, and a passing NIAH score licenses no
conclusion about composition.** It does *not* establish that this model's
positional encoding degrades somewhere between 1k and 8k. Distinguishing
those two needs a model that clears the two-hop bar at short context, so that
the length where it stops clearing it is measurable. This one does not, and no
amount of sweeping fixes that. The results file says so, and
[ADR 0001](docs/adr/0001-why-niah-passes-while-the-task-fails.md) records why
that distinction is the whole judgement call.

The `bridge_found` diagnostic supports the floor reading: the model names the
intermediate entity *less* often than it lands the final answer, meaning it
usually is not attempting the composition at all — it emits some badge number
without first identifying the coordinator. That is a hop-one failure, not
positional decay.

### What each scaling method does to the spectrum

Closed-form algebra over the RoPE frequency ladder — identical on any machine
to the last digit. Head dim 64, base 1,000,000, assumed training context
4,096. `effective_positions` is `θ_scaled / θ_base`: 1.0 means the dimension
was left alone, `1/s` means it was fully interpolated.

| scale | method | fastest pair | slowest pair | logit scale |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 8× | none | 1.0000 | 1.0000 | 1.0000 |
| 8× | pi | 0.1250 | 0.1250 | 1.0000 |
| 8× | ntk | 1.0000 | 0.1250 | 1.0000 |
| 8× | yarn | 1.0000 | 0.1250 | 0.8279 |

Three things this makes exact, all asserted by the script at every scale from
2× to 32×:

- **PI** retains exactly `1/s` of the fastest frequency. Uniform damage, worst
  local resolution, by construction.
- **NTK** retains exactly `1.0000` of it — and this is mechanism, not tuning:
  `θ₀ = base⁰ = 1` for *any* base, so raising the base provably cannot move
  the fastest pair. The `D/(D-2)` exponent is then chosen so the *slowest*
  pair is scaled by exactly `1/s`.
- **All three** converge on `1/s` at the slowest pair. There is no
  disagreement about the low frequencies — that is where range has to come
  from. The methods differ only in what they charge for it.

**YaRN** makes the fast/slow split a rule instead of a side effect of a power
law: at this geometry 7 of 32 pairs complete ≥32 turns in the training window
and are not interpolated at all, 16 complete ≤1 turn and are interpolated
fully like PI, and 9 sit on a linear ramp between. It also multiplies the
logits by `1/t`, `t = 0.1·ln(s)+1` — the term reimplementations drop, and the
only part of YaRN that is not a frequency edit. A test asserts it is present
and that the other methods leave it at exactly 1.0.

## Quickstart

```bash
uv sync --extra dev          # numpy + pytest + ruff. No torch.
uv run pytest -q             # 103 tests
uv run python scripts/generate_results.py
```

Re-running inference is a separate, slower step and needs the model:

```bash
uv sync --extra dev --extra eval
uv run python scripts/run_eval.py      # writes results/eval-cache.json
```

```python
>>> from context_extension_and_long_context_eval.scaling import scaled_rope
>>> pi  = scaled_rope("pi",  scale=8, head_dim=64, train_ctx=4096)
>>> ntk = scaled_rope("ntk", scale=8, head_dim=64, train_ctx=4096)
>>> float(pi.effective_positions[0]), float(ntk.effective_positions[0])
(0.125, 1.0)                     # fastest pair: PI damages it 8x, NTK not at all
>>> float(pi.effective_positions[-1]), float(ntk.effective_positions[-1])
(0.125, 0.12500000000000003)     # slowest pair: both pay the full 1/s
```

Same range extension, same cost at the slow end, and an 8× difference in
retained local resolution. That is the entire argument for NTK over PI in four
numbers.

## Design decisions

**Every scaling method is one function returning a frequency vector.** Not
three classes. They *are* the same intervention, and writing them separately
would hide the only thing that distinguishes them.

**RoPE fixtures use `head_dim=4`.** Two pairs, θ = (1.0, 0.01), so the
expected rotation is a 2×2 matrix checkable with a calculator. A test whose
expected value came out of the implementation proves only that the
implementation is deterministic.

**Both tasks share one scorer, byte for byte.** If NIAH were graded leniently
and multi-hop strictly, the headline gap would be an artefact of the graders.
The shared scorer is what makes the comparison a controlled A/B.

**Multi-hop distractors are complete chains.** Without competing
coordinator+badge pairs, only one badge number exists in the document and "the
badge number" is answerable without hop one — the task silently degenerates
into NIAH. Three tests assert the answer appears nowhere but the second hop,
the bridge entity exactly twice, and every badge distinct. All three would
*inflate* the multi-hop score if broken, which is the direction a test should
watch.

**Inference is in a different script from results generation.** The CI drift
gate regenerates `results/` and byte-compares. If that path loaded the model,
the gate would be testing the runner's network connection. `run_eval.py`
produces a committed cache of accuracies; `generate_results.py` is a pure
function of it and needs only NumPy.

**Accuracies are committed; latency is not.** Greedy decoding over seeded
prompts is a deterministic function of the weights, so the scores regenerate
on another machine. Wall-clock does not, so it goes to a gitignored raw file.
The workspace commit policy was learned the hard way on this exact point.

**The results script asserts its claims and exits non-zero.** Including the
ordering NIAH ≥ multi-hop at every length, the exact frequency-retention
values at every scale, and the disjointness of any interval pair it calls
disjoint.

## Limitations

- **Capability is confounded with context, and here it dominates.** Multi-hop
  is already at 17% at 1k, so this repo does **not** establish a length at
  which composition breaks. It establishes that NIAH does not predict
  composition. Separating the two needs a model that clears the bar at short
  context — out of reach on CPU.
- **The sweep stops far short of the advertised window.** Measured against
  32,768 advertised. Nothing above the longest length run is measured, and no
  value in `results/` is extrapolated into that region.
- **The scaling analysis and the evaluation are not one experiment.**
  `scaling.py` proves closed-form properties; the evaluation runs the model at
  its native RoPE settings. This repo does **not** show YaRN beating PI on the
  multi-hop task — that needs fine-tuning under each method, which there is no
  budget for here.
- **Nothing here measures perplexity or downstream quality under scaling.**
  The scaling results are properties of the *encoding*, not of a trained
  model. A method that looks better on paper can still lose after fine-tuning.
- **LongRoPE is named but not implemented.** Its per-dimension rescale comes
  from evolutionary search against a validation corpus, not a closed form, so
  it cannot join a table of exact algebra.
- **12 samples per cell.** Wide intervals — wide enough to support a
  disjoint-CI claim only where the gap is large, which here it is.
- **Synthetic uniform filler.** Real documents have topical structure a model
  can navigate; removing it makes both tasks harder than their real-world
  equivalents, in the same direction.

## Concepts covered

- **1A RoPE** — rotation formulation, base/theta, why it extrapolates poorly
  (`rope.py`)
- **1A context extension** — position interpolation, NTK-aware, YaRN;
  LongRoPE named and scoped out (`scaling.py`)
- **1A absolute/learned/sinusoidal/ALiBi/NoPE** — implemented for contrast,
  including why ALiBi extrapolates for free and what it pays
  (`alternatives.py`)

  context, lost-in-the-middle depth sweep (`niah.py`, `multihop.py`)
